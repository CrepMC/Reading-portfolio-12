Making Up Thanksgiving as She Went Along

Sarah Lyall   /   2017   /   Personal essay

Can changing a tradition preserve what matters most about it?

CONTENTS01What I read02Evidence & analysis03Explore the idea04Words to keep05My response06Source & connections
01

What I read

Remembering Thanksgivings in England, Lyall describes the freedom to reshape a holiday away from family expectations. She changes the schedule, discards disliked dishes and introduces foods and decorations she enjoys. Playful observations about her English guests create a tone of amused independence. The essay then turns toward a sour-cream apple pie associated with her friend Connie Hays, who died young. What first appears to be a story about inventing traditions becomes a reflection on affection and remembrance. A recipe carries continuity even as the surrounding celebration changes, suggesting that personal meaning can matter more than strict adherence to custom.

READING FOCUS

Track Lyall’s feelings: amused freedom from expectations, pleasure in improvisation, and tenderness when the pie recalls Connie. The emotional shift is supported by the change in focus.

Evidence & analysis

CLAIM

Distance creates room for choice.

EVIDENCE · PARAPHRASEDEarly paragraphs describe changing the celebration’s day and removing unwanted dishes.

WHY IT MATTERSThese details establish independence from inherited expectations without rejecting the gathering itself.

CLAIM

The pie changes the essay’s emotional center.

EVIDENCE · PARAPHRASEDThe dessert section becomes a memory of sharing pie with Connie and receiving her cookbook.

WHY IT MATTERSA concrete food links present celebration to friendship and loss, deepening the earlier humor.

CLAIM

Imperfection can hold meaning.

EVIDENCE · PARAPHRASEDThe final paragraph admits uneven cooking while emphasizing affection associated with the recipe.

WHY IT MATTERSThe conclusion values a relationship over culinary correctness.

03

Explore the idea

Tradition cards

Keep, change, create

Perspective 01

Keep · A shared meal

Lyall keeps gathering people around food and gratitude. The continuity is in connection even when the day, dishes and guests change.

Perspective 02

Change · The rules

She moves the celebration to a convenient day and removes dishes she dislikes. Her playful tone makes adaptation feel liberating rather than like a failure of tradition.

Perspective 03

Create · A personal menu

She introduces desserts and decorations that suit her own tastes. Calling a dish traditional becomes an act of making meaning rather than following an inherited checklist.

Turn the page: why the apple pie matters ↗
The emotional turning point

The pie recalls Connie, a friend who shared it with Lyall during their early reporting careers and later gave her the cookbook. After Connie’s death, making it carries affection and remembrance into a new setting. The essay moves from comic freedom to a tender form of continuity.

05

My response

Lyall’s apple pie makes me think that a tradition can keep its meaning even when the rest of the occasion changes. She moves Thanksgiving to fit life in England and changes the food she serves. Making her friend Connie’s pie still gives her a way to remember their friendship. The form matters when it carries a memory, and that particular recipe does. The other parts of the meal have room to change because their meaning comes from gathering together.
