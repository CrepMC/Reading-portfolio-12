    <div class="section-title"><span>01</span><h2 id="rp-book-5-1-summary-title">What I read</h2></div>
    <p class="summary-text">Rich explores how social media changes the work of musicians and the choices of record labels. Nils Vanderlip’s experience introduces a system in which an existing online audience can determine whether a company listens to an artist at all. TikTok can help unfamiliar songs reach listeners, but maintaining that visibility requires frequent video production, a skill and workload distinct from making music. Accounts of success and burnout complicate any simple celebration of virality. The article also discusses disputes over compensation and platform power, concluding that musical popularity depends on more than the qualities of a song.</p>
      <aside class="reading-focus"><span class="eyebrow">READING FOCUS</span><p>Track how record-label decisions change. The article contrasts listening to demos or live performances with checking follower counts before evaluating music.</p></aside>

    <div class="section-title"><span>05</span><h2 id="rp-book-5-1-reflection-title">My response</h2></div>
    <div class="written-response"><p>I would listen to a musician’s work before looking at follower counts. Rich’s comparison matters because a short clip measures how well a moment travels on a platform. A full song gives a listener time to hear how the artist develops an idea. Audience numbers can still help a label decide where to promote a release. If those numbers determine who gets heard in the first place, a label could miss musicians whose work takes longer to connect with listeners.</p></div>
